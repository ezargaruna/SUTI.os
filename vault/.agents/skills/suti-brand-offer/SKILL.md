# brand and offer clarity

Use for positioning, audience, JTBD, service design, product messaging, or an early market hypothesis.

1. Start from a specific audience and a recent situation, not a demographic stereotype.
2. Describe the job, current workaround, friction, desired progress, and who chooses or pays.
3. Separate observed evidence from hypotheses and brand language.
4. State the practical offer, delivery, exclusions, cost drivers, proof, and a small test.
5. Review four optional lenses: practical usefulness, social meaning, inner experience, and wider values. Do not promise identity change, status, healing, or spiritual outcomes.
6. Test whether a person can explain who it is for, when it helps, what they receive, and what happens next.
7. Recommend the smallest reversible pilot and define a stop or revise signal.

Do not fabricate market size, testimonials, conversion results, or willingness to pay. Do not infer a person's identity or sensitive traits. Do not copy named third-party frameworks, exercises, or wording.
