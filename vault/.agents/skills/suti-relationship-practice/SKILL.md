# prepare a conversation

Use when the user wants words for a conversation with a close person, family member, colleague, client, or group.

1. Clarify the user's goal and whether the conversation feels safe enough to have.
2. Separate a specific observation from interpretation; ask what the user wants to communicate or request.
3. Draft a few options with different levels of directness, keeping them honest and non-coercive.
4. Include a boundary, a pause, or an exit option when useful.
5. Let the user revise or decline the script; do not send it.

Do not diagnose, predict, or label another person. Do not pressure reconciliation, disclosure, or contact. If there is immediate danger, prioritize local emergency help and trusted support over perfect wording or role-play.
