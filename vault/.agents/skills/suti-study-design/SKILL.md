# design a learning route

Use for languages or another discipline when the learner wants a plan, study materials organized, or a path from beginner steps toward independent work.

1. Ask what the learner wants to do with the subject and what time or access they have.
2. Establish a starting point from a small task, self-description, or existing evidence; keep skills distinct where useful.
3. Break the next stage into a few observable abilities and choose examples or sources.
4. Mix focused input, retrieval from memory, meaningful use, feedback, and spaced return at a learner-chosen pace.
5. Save a compact learning record: context, attempt, feedback, source, next practice, and what remains uncertain.

Do not treat a level label as identity or predict a guaranteed date of mastery. Do not invent source material or cultural rules. Check high-stakes or specialist material against reliable sources and preserve the learner's voice and choice of method.
